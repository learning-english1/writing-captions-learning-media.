Quick Learning Guide

A caption is a short text that explains a visual. A good caption helps readers understand what the visual shows and why it is important.

Photograph
Use a factual description. For a completed event, use the past tense.
Example: English Club members performed a drama at the school hall on 20 October 2026.
Picture / Illustration
Describe the subject and credit the creator or source when required.
Example: Mount Bromo, photographed by Rizky Ananda (2025).
Table
Use a clear label and explain what the data measures.
Example: Table 1. Number of Plastic Bottles Collected by Each Grade in One Week.
Graph / Chart
State the trend or the proportions accurately.
Example: Figure 2. The Class's Average English Test Scores from Test 1 to Test 4.

Remember

  • Choose the correct label: Photograph, Picture, Table, Graph, or Chart.
  • Describe only what the visual actually shows.
  • Use the correct tense and include dates or units when needed.
  • Give the source for material taken from another creator or website.
  • Respect privacy. Do not publish identifiable people without permission.
INDIVIDUAL LISTENING

Activity 3 – Listening

Listen to the short audio in which a speaker introduces three visuals: a photograph, a table, and a graph. Complete the notes while listening. You may listen more than once.

Listening audio script

Good morning, everyone. Today I will introduce three visuals about digital life at school. First, the photograph shows students in the computer laboratory at Merdeka Senior High School on Monday, 9 November 2026. They are learning how to create a class presentation using computers. This photograph helps the audience see what digital learning looks like in our school. Second, the table shows the number of students who used the school learning platform during one week. On Monday, 40 students logged in; on Tuesday, fifty students; and on Wednesday, 65 students. The table helps readers compare the number of users on each day. The data was collected by the school's ICT team. Third, the line graph shows the number of students who submitted their English assignments online over four weeks. The number increased from 20 students in Week 1 to 35 in Week 2, 45 in Week 3, and 60 in Week 4. The graph makes the upward trend easy to see. The table and graph use data collected by the school's ICT team in November 2026. Thank you.

Use the button to listen. If audio playback is not supported on your device, ask your teacher to read the script aloud.

Activity 7 – Group Project: Caption Folder

Theme: Digital Life at School

Work in a group of four. Collect one photograph, one picture, one table, one graph, and one chart. Write a caption for each visual, check the accuracy of the data, include the source, and ask permission when people appear in a photograph.

GROUP PRESENTATION

Activity 8 – Presentation

Record a one-to-two-minute video or audio presentation of your caption folder, or present it live.

Presentation steps

  1. Introduce your theme: Digital Life at School.
  2. Show each visual on the screen.
  3. Read its caption aloud.
  4. Explain why the caption is accurate and useful.
  5. Answer one audience question about the social function of captions.
